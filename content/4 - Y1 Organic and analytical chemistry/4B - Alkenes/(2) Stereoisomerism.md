In alkenes, the restricted rotation around the $\pi$-bond can cause the molecule to have two different isomers. These are stereoisomers.

>[!QUOTE] Stereoisomerism describes molecules with the same structural formula, but a different arrangement of atoms in 3D space.

Stereoisomerism is distinct to [[(4) Structural isomerism#Structural isomers|structural isomerism]] in that stereoisomers have the same [[(2) Representing organic molecules#Structural formula|structural formula]].

>[!QUESTION] Exam question: Why are alkenes able to exhibit stereoisomerism? (2 marks)
>- There is restricted rotation around the C=C double bond (1 mark).
>- Each C atom of the C=C double bond is bonded to two different types of group (1 mark)

### E-Z isomerism
E/Z isomerism is an example of stereoisomerism. It is also known as geometric isomerism. It has two requirements:
1. Restricted rotation about a double bond.
2. **Two different groups** to be attached to each carbon atom of the C=C group.

#### Identifying *E* and *Z* stereoisomers
The Cahn-Ingold-Prelog (CIP) rules are used to assign priority to one of the two groups attached to each C in the C=C double bond.

1. Look at each C in the C=C at a time
2. List the atoms attached directly to the C in the C=C. The larger atomic number is the higher number.
3. If the two atoms are the same, then list the atomic numbers of the atom one bond further out from the C=C from the largest to smallest. Compare the numbers one by one, the first group to contain a higher number is the priority group.
4. Draw an imaginary line along the double bond. If both priority groups are on the same side of the double bond, it is the *Z* isomer, if not it is the *E* isomer.

### Cis-trans isomerism

Cis-trans isomerism is a special case of E/Z isomerism, in which two of the substituent groups attached to each carbon atom of the C=C group are the same.

#### Identifying cis and trans isomers
The "cis" (*same*) isomer is where two identical groups are on the same side of the C=C bond. The "trans" (*different*) isomer is where two identical groups are on opposite sides of the C=C bond.

The "E" is equivalent to the trans side, and "Z" is equivalent to the cis side, however this is only correct when there is an H on each carbon atom of the C=C bond.

| Relative position of relevant functional groups | E-Z isomerism | Cis-trans isomerism |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------- |
| Same side of the double bond                    | Z             | Cis                 |
| Opposite side of the double bond                | E             | Trans               |
