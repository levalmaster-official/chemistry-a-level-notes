>[!QUOTE] Addition reaction
>A reaction where a reactant is added to an unsaturated molecule to make one saturated molecule

The two new groups of atoms always attach to the carbons that were in the double bond.  Electrophilic addition is the type of reaction taking place when small molecules saturate double bonds in alkenes, e.g. $\ce{HBr}$ and $\ce{C2H4}$. In the following reaction, the small molecule acts as an electrophile ($\ce{E+}$). An electrophile is an **electron pair acceptor**. The mechanism of electrophilic addition in alkenes is by [[(5) Reaction mechanisms#Heterolytic fission|heterolytic fission]].

Different addition reactions have different reactants or reagents, conditions and products. Reactions between non-symmetrical alkenes and non-symmetrical reactants produce two products.
#### Reaction mechanism

1. The double bond in the alkene is electron rich and [[(6) Sigma and pi bonds#Reactions involving sigma-bonds|attracts electrophiles]].
2. The $\pi$-bond in the C=C breaks. The pair of electrons from the $\pi$-bond makes a new bond to the $\ce{\delta+}$ atom in the electrophile.
3. The $\ce{\delta+}$ atom has too many bonds, so the polar bond in the electrophile breaks and the electrons move onto the $\ce{\delta-}$ atom as a lone pair.
4. The bond is broken by heterolytic fission, forming two ions. The organic ion is known as a **carbocation**.
5. A bond forms from the lone pair on the -ve ion to the +ve carbon atom in the carbocation, forming the product.

>[!TIP] Tips for drawing reaction mechanisms
>1. Ensure that the $\ce{\delta+}$ atom is closer to the C=C double bond
>2. The electrophile should be drawn vertically
>3. Partial charges ($\ce{\delta+, \delta-}$) and full charges (+, -) must be shown
>4. Curly arrows must go precisely to and from the right places

  
Sometimes the electrophile concerned is not polar (e.g. $\ce{Br2, H2}$). Here, the double bond polarises the electrophile, allowing them to react together.
