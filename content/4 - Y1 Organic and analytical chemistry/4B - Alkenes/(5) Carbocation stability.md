### Carbocation classification
[[(4) Electrophilic addition#Reaction mechanism|Carbocations]] can be classified as being primary, secondary or tertiary.

| Primary                                                                           | Secondary                                                                         | Tertiary                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Positive charge is on a carbon attached to one alkyl group and two hydrogen atoms | Positive charge is on a carbon attached to two alkyl groups and one hydrogen atom | Positive charge is on a carbon attached to three alkyl groups and no hydrogen atoms |

### Carbocation stability
When both the alkene and the electrophile are asymmetric, two products can be formed. Here, two carbocations are able to be drawn. one for each of the possible products, e.g. for propene and $\ce{HBr}$.

![[Pasted image 20250326011633.png]]

These two (reasonably) unstable carbocation intermediates are not equally likely to form. The more stable carbocation intermediate (the more substituted one) will be more likely to form. Since that intermediate will be more likely to collide with the bromide ion, one of the products will be more likely to form; this product is known as the **major organic product**. 

1. The two carbocations formed have different stabilities.
2. The **most stable carbocation** is the one where the **positive charge is on the carbon with the most alkyl groups attached**, i.e. the carbocation with the highest classification.
3. The product formed by the most stable carbocation is known as the major product and makes up the majority of the total product formed. The other product is known as the minor product.


>[!EXAMPLE] Exam-style question: why is 2-bromopropane the major product?
>- 2-bromopropane is the major product because it forms from the more stable secondary carbocation intermediate.
>- 1-bromopropane is the minor product because it forms from the less stable primary carbocation intermediate.
