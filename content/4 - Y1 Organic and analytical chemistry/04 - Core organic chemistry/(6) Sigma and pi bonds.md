### Bond enthalpy
Bond enthalpy is the energy needed to break one mole of a particular bond. A C=C double bond has a much higher bond enthalpy (more than 2x) than a C-C single bond. As it is **more** than double, this shows that the two bonds in a C=C double bond are not the same and one of the bonds is weaker than the other.

### Bonding
Covalent bonds are formed when orbitals on two different atoms overlap. When the orbitals of two atoms overlap head-on, a direct overlap results, producing a hybrid orbital.
### Sigma bonds
Between the two atoms, the direct overlap of orbitals produces a sigma ($\sigma$) bond. When single bonds form. they are always sigma bonds.

>[!QUOTE] Sigma bond
>Overlap of orbitals directly between the bonding (C) atoms

![[Pasted image 20250113120812.png]]
Sigma bonds are stronger than pi-bonds. They have free rotation, whereas pi-bonds are unable to rotate.

>[!QUESTION] Why are sigma-bonds stronger than pi-bonds?
>- Shared electron pair in a sigma bond is closer to the positively charged nuclei of both atoms, meaning a stronger force of attraction between the electrons (negatively charged) and nuclei (positively charged).
>- Shared electron pair in a pi-bond is further from the nuclei of both atoms, meaning a weaker force of attraction between the electrons and nuclei.
### Pi bonds
Covalent bonds can also form when adjacent p- or d- [[(2) Electronic configuration#The orbital angular momentum quantum number|orbitals]] of the two atoms overlap sideways. This produces a pi ($\pi$) bond.

>[!QUOTE] Pi bond
>Sideways overlap of adjacent p-orbitals above and below the bonding (C) atoms

![[Pasted image 20250113120547.png]]

Two atoms that form a sigma and pi-bond between themselves will have a double bond between them, and will be harder to break apart than if they only had sigma bond.
#### Rotation in sigma-bonds
In order for the p-orbitals of the atoms to overlap, they must be parallel. If the bond was rotated, the p-orbitals making up the $\pi-$ bond would no longer overlap, and the $\pi-$bond would break. Since this would require a lot of energy, the double bond is said to have **restricted** or **fixed** rotation. This property can lead to [[(2) Stereoisomerism|stereoisomerism]].
#### Reactions involving sigma-bonds
Sigma bonds have two regions of electron density. Their relatively high electron density make them vulnerable to attacks by electrophiles, i.e. they participate in [[(3) Addition reactions of alkenes|addition reactions ]]with electron-deficient species, such as halogens.
