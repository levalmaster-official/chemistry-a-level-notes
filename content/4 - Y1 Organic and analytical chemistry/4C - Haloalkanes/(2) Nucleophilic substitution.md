Haloalkenes react in [[(2) Reactions of alkanes#Substitution reactions|substitution]] reactions to make alcohols and amines. [[(1) Naming alcohols|Alcohols]], amines, and halo groups can be interchanged using this reaction.

$$\ce{CH_{3}CH_{2}Cl + NaOH -> CH_{3}CH_{2}OH + NaCl}$$
##### Nucleophiles
A nucleophile is added to a haloalkane and is substituted with the halogen atom. A nucleophile ($\ce{:Nu}$ or $\ce{:Nu-}$) is an atom (or group of atoms) which is attracted to an electron deficient centre. Common examples include $\ce{NH_{3}}$ or $\ce{H_{2}O}$. They are the opposite of an [[(4) Electrophilic addition|electrophile]].

>[!QUOTE] Nucleophile
>An electron pair donor

#### Hydrolysis reaction
This reaction is known as a hydrolysis reaction, and it needs to be heated over an extended period of time in order to proceed. In order to do this without losing all the liquid as vapour, it is necessary to use an experimental procedure known as heating under reflux.
#### Reflux
Reflux is a method to allow the continuous boiling of the reaction mixture without it boiling dry. The reactants/solvent/products vaporise, rise up the condenser, then cool, condense and drip back down into the reacting flask.

>[!QUOTE] Reflux
>The continual boiling and condensing back to the original container to ensure that the reaction takes place without the contents of the flask boiling dry

We use reflux when heating volatile or flammable substances (reactants/products).
