### Ozone
Ozone ($\ce{O_{3}}$) protects the surface of the earth by absorbing harmful high energy UV radiation.

Ozone is formed from oxygen in the lower stratosphere:
$$\ce{O_{2} ->[UV] O^{.} + O^{.}}$$
$$\ce{O^{.} + O_{2} -> O_{3}}$$
Ozone absorbs high energy UV, so some ozone is also reaction away:
$$\ce{O_{3} ->[UV] O_{2} + O}$$
$$\ce{O_{3} + O^{.} -> O_{2} + O_{2}}$$
Left undisturbed, this cycle continues, maintaining a roughly fixed quantity of ozone in the atmosphere.
### Chlorofluorocarbons
Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) are molecules made up of only chlorine, fluorine and carbon.

In the stratosphere, the UV radiation is high enough energy to break $\ce{C-Cl}$ bonds [[(5) Reaction mechanisms#Homolytic fission|homolytically]], making chlorine radicals. Chlorine radicals pose a significantly hazard as they catalyse the breakdown of ozone.

$$
\ce{Initiation: CF_{2}Cl_{2} -> CF_{2}Cl^{.} + ^{.}Cl}
$$
$$
\ce{Propagation: Cl + O_{3} -> ^{.}ClO + O_{2}}
$$
$$
\ce{ClO + O -> ^{.}Cl + O_{2}}
$$
$$
\ce{Overall: O_{3} + O -> 2O_{2}}
$$
To avoid this, we now use HCFCs, where the $\ce{C-H}$ bond is weak enough to break before the molecules reach the stratosphere.
