Specification reference: [[OCR A Chemistry Specification#Reactions of alcohols|Reactions of alcohols]]

Alcohols can react to form [[(1) Structure of alkenes|alkenes]]. This is the opposite of [[(6) Addition polymerisation|hydration]], which is why elimination of $\ce{H_{2}O}$ from alcohols is also known as dehydration. Certain conditions are required:

- An acid catalyst (e.g. concentrated $\ce{H_{2}SO_{4}}$ or $\ce{H_{3}PO_{4}}$)
- Heated under [[(2) Nucleophilic substitution#Hydrolysis reaction|reflux]]
$$\ce{
CH_{2}(OH)CH_{2}CH_{3} -> CH_{2}CHCH_{3} + H_{2}O
}$$
![[dehydration-of-alcohol.png| 500]]
The alcohol group is lost, together with a H atom from an adjacent carbon atom

The C=C double bond will always include the carbon that the $\ce{-OH}$ group was attached to. Depending on the position of the alcohol group, more than one product might be formed.
