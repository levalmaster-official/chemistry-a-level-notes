[[(1) Naming alcohols|Alcohols]] can react with [[(5) Oxidation of alcohols#Primary alcohols|carboxylic acids]] to form esters. The conditions for this reactions are:
- Concentration $\ce{H_{2}SO_{4}}$ catalyst
- Heating under reflux

The reaction is reversible. A hydrogen comes from the alcohol, and a hydroxyl group comes from the carboxylic acid to form a water molecule. This is a condensation reaction.
##### Uses of esters
One notable property of ester is that they frequently have a sweet, fruity smell. They are often used in perfumes and flavourings.

#### Naming esters
1. The side of the ester group attached the the single bonded oxygen (from the alcohol) is named first. It gets the suffix "-yl". The carbon attached to the oxygen is always C1 when numbered on this side.
2. The side of the ester group attached to the $\ce{C=O}$ (from the carboxylic acid) is named second. It gets the suffix "-oate". The C is the $\ce{C=O}$ counts as part of the chain and is always C1 when numbering on this side

A benzene ring as a side chain, e.g. with $\ce{-O-}$ is called "phenyl". A benzene ring on the $\ce{C=O}$ is known as "benzoate". For example, the below molecules are phenyl methanoate and 2-methylbutyl benzoate.

```smiles
C1=CC=C(C=C1)OC=O
CCC(C)COC(=O)C1=CC=CC=C1
```
