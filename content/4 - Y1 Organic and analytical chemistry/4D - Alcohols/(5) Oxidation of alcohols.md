#### Oxidising agent
Alcohols can be [[(3) Reduction and oxidation|oxidised]] to form new [[(3) Functional groups|functional groups]]. Depending on whether the alcohol group is [[(1) Naming alcohols|primary, secondary or tertiary]], different products will be formed.

The oxidising agent \[O] generally used is **acidified potassium dichromate (VI)**, $\ce{H+/Cr_{2}O_{7}^{2+}}$ which can also be written as $\ce{H_{2}SO_{4}/K_{2}Cr_{2}O_{7}}$, which is an <span style="color:orange">orange</span> solution.

The chromium in the dichromate is reduced (its oxidation number decreases). It forms <span style="color:green">green</span> $\ce{Cr^{3+}}$ ions or sometimes <span style="color:blue">blue</span> $\ce{Cr^2+}$ ions.
$$\ce{
14H+ + Cr_{2}O_{7}^{2-} + 6e- -> 2Cr^{3+} + 7H_{2}O
}$$
#### Primary alcohols
Primary alcohols can be oxidised to form [[(1) Naming organic molecules#Aldehyde (-CHO)|aldehydes]] and [[(1) Naming organic molecules#Carboxylic acid (-COOH)|carboxylic acids]]. The oxidation product can be controlled by using *different* reaction conditions.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ce{
Partial oxidation: CH_{3}CH_{2}OH + [O] -> CH_{3}CHO + H_{2}O} \\[3mm]
\ce{Full oxidation: CH_{3}CH_{2}OH + 2[O] -> CH_{3}COOH + H_{2}O}
\end{aligned}
$$
The aldehyde must be distilled as it is formed to prevent further oxidation to a carboxylic acid.

#### Secondary alcohols
Secondary alcohols can be oxidised to form [[(1) Naming organic molecules#Ketone (-C(=O)-)|ketones]].

$$\ce{
CH_{3}CH(OH)CH_{3} + [O] -> CH_{3}COCH_{3} + H_{2}O
}$$
The reaction is heated under [[(2) Nucleophilic substitution#Reflux|reflux]] to make sure the oxidation proceeds with the maximum yield.
#### Tertiary alcohols
Tertiary alcohols do not oxidise, and hence have no reaction with acidified potassium dichromate (VI).
