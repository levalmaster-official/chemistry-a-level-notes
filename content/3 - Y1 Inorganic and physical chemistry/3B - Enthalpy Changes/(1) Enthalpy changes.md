Enthalpy is the measure of the heat energy in a chemical system. Enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) is the difference in enthalpy between the reactants and products in a chemical reaction.

If the enthalpy of the reactants is higher than the products, then energy will be released into the surroundings as heat. These reactions are known as **exothermic**. $\Delta H$ for these reactions is negative.

If the enthalpy of the reactants is lower than the products, then energy will be taken in from the surroundings. These reactions are known as **endothermic**. $\Delta H$ for these reactions is positive.

>[!SUMMARY] Making bonds is exothermic; breaking bonds is endothermic.

#### Enthalpy profile diagrams
These show the relative enthalpies of the reactants and products in a reaction.

#### Enthalpy definitions
All enthalpy definitions are under standard conditions and standard states (physical states under standard conditions).

Standard conditions are defined as following:

| Temperature  | Pressure       | Concentration        |
| ------------ | -------------- | -------------------- |
| $\ce{298 K}$ | $\ce{100 kPa}$ | $\ce{1 mol dm}^{-3}$ |
Standard state refers to the most stable physical state of a substance under standard conditions.
###### Enthalpy change of reactions
$\Delta_{r}H$ is the enthalpy change that accompanies a reaction in the molar quantities shown in a chemical equation under standard conditions
###### Enthalpy change of combustion
$\Delta_{c}H$ is the enthalpy change of the complete combustion of 1 mol of a substance (it reacts completely with oxygen) under standard conditions.
###### Enthalpy change of formation
$\ce{\Delta_{f}H}$ is the enthalpy change of the formation of 1 mol of a compound from its constituent elements under standard conditions.
###### Enthalpy change of neutralisation
$\Delta_{neut}H$ is the enthalpy change of neutralisation. This is the formation of 1 mol of water from the neutralisation reaction of an acid with a base under standard conditions.
