The rate of a chemical reaction describes how fast reactants are used up or products are formed.  It is defined as the change in concentration of reactants or products in a given time, and has units $\text{mol dm}^{-3} \text{ s}^{-1}$.
$$
\text{Rate of reaction} = \frac{\text{Change in concentration /mol dm}^{-3}}{\text{Time taken /s}}
$$
### Collision theory
For a reaction to occur:
1. Particles must collide in the correct orientation
2. Particles must also collide with enough energy to overcome the [[(1) Enthalpy changes#Enthalpy profile diagrams|activation energy]] barrier (to break the bonds)
It follows that we can increase the rate of reactions if we increase the frequency of collisions or the energy of collisions.
#### Reaction conditions
##### Effect of concentration
If we increase the concentration of a reactant, there are more particles in the same volume so they will collide more frequently.
##### Effect of pressure
If the pressure of a gaseous substance increases, there will be more gas particles per unit volume, so they will collide more frequently.
##### Surface area
A greater surface area will mean that more particles are available to react for a given mass, so collisions will occur more frequently.
##### Effect of temperature
Heating the reaction mixture will give the particles more kinetic energy. 
- The particles will move faster, so they will collide more frequently, increasing the rate of reaction.
- As particles have more kinetic energy, they are also more likely to exceed the activation energy on collision.

>A greater proportion of particles having energy that exceeds the activation energy has a greater effect than particles colliding more frequently
