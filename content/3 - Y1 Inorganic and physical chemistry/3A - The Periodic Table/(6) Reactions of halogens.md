#### Reactions of halogens
Halogens have a $\ce{s^{2}p^5}$ electron configuration. They gain one electron in many redox reactions to form 1- ions. 
#### Appearance of the halogens in solution

| Solvent       | $\ce{Cl2}$      | $\ce{Br2}$ | $\ce{I2}$ |
| ------------- | --------------- | ---------- | --------- |
| (Cyclo)hexane | Very pale green | Orange     | Purple    |
| Water         | Very pale green | Orange     | Brown     |
#### Displacement reactions
In a displacement reaction, a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.
$$
\ce{X2 + 2Y- -> 2X- + Y2}
$$
X is the more reactive element because it reacted to form a compound. Y is less reactive because it is less able to stay in the compound form than X.

A reactivity series for the halides can be seen using displacement reactions:

|            | $\ce{Cl2}$         | $\ce{Br2}$           | $\ce{I2}$ |
| ---------- | ------------------ | -------------------- | --------- |
| $\ce{Cl2}$ |                    | $\ce{Br2}$ is formed | $\ce{I2}$ is formed |
| $\ce{Br2}$ | $\ce{Br2}$ remains |                      | $\ce{I2}$ is formed |
| $\ce{I2}$  | $\ce{I2}$ remains  | $\ce{I2}$ remains    |           |
#### Trend in reactivity for halogens
The halogens decrease in reactivity down the group.
- The halogens react by gaining an electron (reducing).
- The atoms further down the group are bigger and the outer shell experiences more electron shielding.
- Halogens further down the group are less able to attract an electron.
- Oxidising strength decreases down the group.

### Disproportionation reactions of chlorine
Chorine undergoes [[(3) Reduction and oxidation#In terms of changes in oxidation number|disproportionation]] reactions with water and with sodium hydroxide.

1. Reaction of chlorine with **water**
$$
\ce{Cl2 + H2O -> HCl + HClO}
$$
Chlorine is used in the purification of mains water. Chloric (I) acid can oxidise bacterial cell walls and them down. However, chlorine gas was used as a chemical weapon in the trenches in WW1. The $\ce{HCl}$ produced corroded mucous membranes. $\ce{HClO}$ kills bacteria but is not very stable. $\ce{NaClO}$, sodium chlorate (I) salt is much more stable, so chorine can be reacted with sodium hydroxide instead.

2. Reaction of chlorine with **sodium hydroxide**
$$
\ce{Cl2 + 2NaOH -> NaCl + NaClO + H2O}
$$
Household bleach can be made by reacting chlorine with cold, dilute, aqueous sodium hydroxide.
