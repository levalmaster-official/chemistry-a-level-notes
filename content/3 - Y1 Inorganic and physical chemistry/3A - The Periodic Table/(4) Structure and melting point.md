Specification reference: [[OCR A Chemistry Specification#Periodic trend in structure and melting point|Periodic trend in structure and melting point]]

The melting point is affected by the structure and bonding present in the element. It depends on strength of attractions holding individual particles together (which need to be broken).

It is governed by:
- The type of the bonds or intermolecular forces that need to be broken to melt the substance
- The strength of these bonds or forces

### Exam-style questions
>[!EXAMPLE] Describe and explain the general trend of melting points across Period 2
>- Li and Be have giant metallic lattice structures, held together by strong metallic bonds (the electrostatic attraction between positive metal cations and their delocalised outer electrons) which require a large about of energy to break.  Be has a higher melting point than Li as more energy is required to break the stronger metallic bonds due to the cations having a higher charge.
>- B and C have giant covalent lattice structures, held together by strong covalent bonds which require a large amount of energy to break.  C has a higher boiling point than B as C forms 4 bonds per atom whereas B forms only 3, the bonds between C atoms are also slightly stronger, requiring more energy to break.
>- $\ce{N2}$,$\ce{O2}$ and $\ce{F2}$ and have simple covalent molecular structures, Ne exists as simple atoms.  The weak London forces between the molecules/atoms all require little energy to break, leading to all the substances having low melting points.  Ne is the lowest as it has the least electrons per atom/molecule.
