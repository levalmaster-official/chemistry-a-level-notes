In redox reactions, electrons are being transferred from one species (an atom, ion, or molecule) to another, so both reduction and oxidation occur.
#### In terms of electron transfer
- A species that has gained electrons is said to have been <span style="color:lightcoral">reduced</span>.
- A species that has lost electrons is said to have been <span style="color:lightblue">oxidised</span>.
##### Oxidising and reducing agents
- The species responsible for oxidising another is called the **oxidising agent**. It contains the element that is reduced.
- The species responsible for reducing another is called the **reducing agent**. It contains the element that is oxidised.

#### In terms of changes in oxidation number
- An element is <span style="color:lightblue">oxidised</span> if its [[(4) Oxidation numbers|oxidation number]] increases.
- An element is <span style="color:lightcoral">reduced</span> if its oxidation number decreases.

[[(6) Reactions of halogens#Disproportionation reactions of chlorine|Disproportionation]] is a redox reaction where the same element is oxidised and reduced.
#### Redox reactions of metals with acids to form salts
Dilute acids undergo redox reactions with some metals to produce salts and hydrogen gas.
$$
\ce{metal + acid -> salt + hydrogen}
$$
In these reactions, the metal is oxidised and the acid is reduced.
