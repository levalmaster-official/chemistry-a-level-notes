Specification reference: [[OCR A Chemistry Specification#Oxidation number|2.1.5 Oxidation number]]

Oxidation number is the number of electrons an atom uses to bond with atoms of another element. 

>[!INFO] Ionic? I think not!
 > The oxidation number is the hypothetical charge of an atom if all of its bonds to other atoms were fully ionic. 
 > - This is because even in an ionic compound such as $\ce{NaCl}$ there are some covalent interactions occurring.
 > - Therefore, even covalently bonded atoms can have oxidation 
#### Rules of assigning and calculation oxidation numbers
1.  Atoms of uncombined elements always have an oxidation number of 0.
2. Atoms of a Group I element have an oxidation number of +1
3. Atoms of a Group II element have an oxidation number of +2
4. Fluorine atoms have an oxidation number of -1
5. Oxygen atoms have an oxidation number of -2, except when in a peroxide (e.g. $\ce{H2O2, K2O2}$ etc.) then it is -1
6. Hydrogen atoms have an oxidation of +1, except when in a hydride (e.g.  $\ce{LiH, NaBH4}$ , etc.) then it is -1
7. The sum of the oxidation numbers in a compound is equal to the total charge of said compound

#### Roman numerals in naming
The Roman numeral shows the oxidation state of the element without a sign (the magnitude) when an element may have compounds/ions with different oxidation numbers.
